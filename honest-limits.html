Honest Limits — What V1 Is Not

An honest scope list for version 1.0.0, written down so you can plan around it instead of discovering it.

  • Not a live platform integration. The Steam and Epic adapters ship with a transport interface and a mock transport, verified against the mock only. No platform SDK is bundled, no native calls are made, and no live sync was tested — live verification needs your partner app ID and your SDK.
  • Not a dialogue system. Quests reference conversations through flags your game sets (“spoke to the gatekeeper”); the conversations themselves belong to your dialogue tooling.
  • Not an inventory or economy system. Rewards report intent through your reward sink. Your inventory and economy apply them; the tracker never owns item state beyond an optional item-count view for conditions.
  • Not a translation engine. Localization-ready means every player-facing string carries a key and resolves through one replaceable resolver. Translating the strings is your localization pipeline's job.
  • Not visual scripting for gameplay logic. The tree editor authors quest structure. What your game does when an objective target is reported stays your code.
  • No multiplayer authority model. The tracker is a plain C# object; which side of a network session owns it, and how its snapshots replicate, is your architecture's decision.
  • No quest content beyond the starter quests and the demo. They teach the system and prove it end to end. Your game's quests are the content; this package is the system they run on.
  • Not a save system. Snapshots are yours to store — the tracker produces and consumes them and ships two convenience stores, but autosave policy, slots UI and corruption recovery belong to your game.
