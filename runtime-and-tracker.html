Runtime & the Tracker

The one-minute model

  • A catalog lists your quest and achievement definitions.
  • A QuestTracker is constructed over a catalog plus a reward sink. It owns all live state; definitions are never mutated at runtime.
  • Your game reports events — progress on a target id, a flag set, a stat change. The tracker routes each report to every active objective and progress achievement listening for it, then settles the graphs.

How nodes advance (the pump)

After every report, the tracker pumps each affected quest to a fixed point: completed objective nodes grant their rewards (exactly once, in order) and activate their successors; branch nodes evaluate their conditions; End nodes complete the quest. The pump is bounded (256 steps per quest per mutation) — a graph that cannot settle is a content bug and stops with a warning, never an infinite loop.

Reporting events

tracker.AddProgress("ember_shard");                 // collect-style targets
tracker.AddProgress(QuestObjectiveKind.Interact, "gate_lever");
tracker.SetFlag("spoke_to_gatekeeper");
tracker.AddStat("reputation", 1);
tracker.StartQuest("first_embers");
tracker.FailQuest("first_embers", "The forge burned down.");

Events

The tracker raises plain C# events your UI (or the included UI kit) binds to: quest available / started / completed / failed, node completed, objective progressed, achievement unlocked / progressed, and state restored. Errors are state and events — never exceptions across your game loop.

Quest states

Locked → Available → Active → Completed / Failed. Unlock conditions on a quest definition (for example “First Steps is complete”) are re-evaluated whenever the tracker changes; the demo's policy of starting a quest the moment it unlocks is one line — your game chooses its own start policy, or uses the tracker's auto-start.
